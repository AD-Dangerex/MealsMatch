05 — HOW MATCHING WORKS

Eligibility first.
Then a transparent score.

Requests and listings must meet essential conditions before any recommendation is shown. The score ranks eligible pairs; it does not guarantee a safe or successful handover.

GATE 01

Mandatory eligibility checks

  • Food category and quantity unit must match.
  • Food must remain available until the request deadline.
  • Pickup windows must overlap.
  • Dietary needs and excluded allergens are checked against donor-entered details.
  • Required storage must be available to the recipient.
  • The donor must confirm their handling declaration.

06 — BETTER HANDOVERS

Useful details make
coordination easier.

Each listing and request captures practical information so people and organisations can decide whether a match works for them before confirming an allocation.

01

Food and quantity

Category, name, amount, unit, dietary tags, allergen details, and packaging notes make listings more descriptive.

02

Storage and timing

Storage method, preparation time, use-by time, and pickup windows help participants assess practical fit.

03

Recipient context

Recipient type, expected people supported, dietary needs, storage capacity, and urgency add useful request context.

04

Private contact

Phone details are kept off public listings and are shared with the matched participant only after an allocation.

Important: FoodBridge is a coordination tool, not a food-safety authority.

Food details and handling declarations are supplied by users. The platform does not inspect, test, certify, or guarantee food. Donors and recipients remain responsible for checking condition, traceability, allergens, storage, transport, and local legal requirements before a handover.

07 — COMMON QUESTIONS

Clear expectations.
Better collaboration.

Who can create an account?

Individuals and organisations can register as a food donor or recipient. Each account chooses one role; to use both workflows, create a separate account for each. Admin accounts are created separately by the site operator.

How are recommendations ranked?

Ineligible pairs are removed first. Eligible matches receive a weighted score based on quantity, compatibility, city, timing, urgency, and recent allocation history. Open “See match score breakdown” in a dashboard to inspect a specific result.

Does a high score mean the food is safe?

No. A score reflects only the data entered into the system. It is not a safety inspection or certification, and the participating organisations must verify all details themselves.

How are quantities and privacy handled?

Matching compares like units only. Public pages show aggregate platform counts; contact numbers and pickup instructions are restricted to participants in a recorded allocation.
